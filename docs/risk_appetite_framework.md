# Risk appetite and early-warning framework

The risk-appetite framework translates model outputs into explicit management thresholds and
escalation actions. It separates three related questions:

1. Is aggregate portfolio risk within approved capacity?
2. Are concentrations, credit quality and pricing moving towards unacceptable levels?
3. Which individual deals require immediate review or enhanced monitoring?

## Portfolio scorecard

Eight measures cover loss-at-risk, Expected Shortfall, expected-loss intensity, premium coverage,
largest country and sector shares, country HHI and the share of covered exposure in IFRS 9 Stages 2
and 3. Each measure has a Green/Amber/Red threshold and a predefined management action.

Red means the metric is outside appetite and requires immediate owner review. Amber is an early
warning requiring named ownership and monthly monitoring. Green remains within appetite. Headroom
is measured against the Red threshold so decision-makers can see proximity to a breach.

## Deal early-warning register

The deal register evaluates five trigger families:

- point-in-time probability of default;
- IFRS 9 stage;
- country-limit utilisation;
- premium coverage of the expected-loss rate; and
- illustrative country risk grade.

Multiple triggers remain visible rather than being collapsed into a single opaque score. A deal is
Red if any Red trigger fires and Amber if one or more Amber triggers fire without a Red trigger.

## Escalation protocol

For every Red item, the owner should record whether the response is to accept, mitigate, transfer or
decline the risk, together with rationale, approver and review date. Amber items should have a named
owner, monitoring frequency and exit criteria. Material breaches should be included in the next
portfolio committee pack and escalated immediately when delay could increase loss.

All limits in this repository are fictional and illustrative. Production thresholds require formal
approval, calibration against loss experience and capital capacity, independent challenge and
controlled change management.
